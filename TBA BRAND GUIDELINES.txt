## 1. BRAND FOUNDATION

### 1.1 — Brand Name
**Full Name:** Theropod Basketball Association  
**Short Name:** TBA  
**Stylised:** TBA. *(the period is intentional — it signals finality, dominance — as in: the matter is settled)*  
**Never write as:** The TBA, T.B.A., tba

### 1.2 — Brand Mission
> To engineer performance footwear that turns athletes into apex predators — on the court and in their mindset.

### 1.3 — Brand Vision
> A world where every serious baller is equipped to dominate — where the court is your territory, and you defend it.

### 1.4 — Brand Values

| Value | What It Means for TBA |
|---|---|
| **Apex Performance** | Everything we make is engineered to be the best. No compromise, no excuses. |
| **Earned Dominance** | You don't get to the top by accident. TBA is for athletes who put in the work. |
| **Predator Mentality** | Attack the game. Attack improvement. Attack complacency. |
| **Evolved Design** | Like the species we draw from, we evolve — innovation is survival. |
| **Court is Territory** | The game is not just physical. It's territorial, psychological, strategic. |

### 1.5 — Brand Positioning Statement
**For** serious basketball athletes who demand peak performance from every piece of gear, **TBA** is the footwear brand that **channels the raw, engineered dominance of prehistoric apex predators** — because **real ballers don't compete to participate. They compete to extinct the competition.**

### 1.6 — Taglines

**Primary Tagline:**  
> **EVOLVED TO DOMINATE.**

**Secondary Taglines (use contextually):**
- *"The apex is earned, not given."*
- *"Your court. Your territory. Your era."*
- *"They went extinct. You won't."*
- *"Built from the bones of something unstoppable."*
- *"66 million years in the making."*

## 2. BRAND STORY & NARRATIVE

### 2.1 — The Origin Story (Long Form)
*Use for website About pages, press kits, investor decks, and onboarding materials.*

---

The Spinosaurus didn't look like what you'd expect from the largest carnivore that ever lived. It wasn't built like its cousins. It hunted differently. Moved differently. Evolved to survive — and to dominate — in ways no one anticipated.

That's TBA.

We didn't set out to make another basketball shoe. We set out to rethink what a basketball shoe could be — from the sole up, from the science out, from the court into the culture.

TBA was born from one belief: **that serious athletes deserve gear as evolved as they are.** Not lifestyle. Not hype. Not a logo slapped on foam. Performance architecture. Predator engineering.

Our primary inspiration is the Spinosaurus — the apex of apex. A theropod that redefined what dominance looks like. Our antagonist is the Therizinosaurus — arms like blades, slow as doubt, dangerous if you let it get close. It is every obstacle. Every opponent who slept on you. Every coach who cut you.

You are the Spinosaurus.

The Therizinosaurus is everything standing between you and the game you're supposed to play.

**TBA exists to make sure you're ready.**

---

### 2.2 — The Origin Story (Short Form)
*Use for social bios, email footers, one-pagers, and ad copy.*

---

TBA makes performance basketball footwear for athletes who operate at the top of the food chain. Inspired by the Spinosaurus — the most dominant predator to ever walk the earth — and engineered to outlast everything standing in your way.

**Evolved to Dominate.**

---

### 2.3 — The Narrative Tension (Core Brand Conflict)
Every great brand has conflict. TBA's is primal:

**The Hero:** *You* — the serious baller. Channeling the Spinosaurus. Dominant, evolved, relentless.  
**The Antagonist:** The Therizinosaurus — representing the opposition. Slow-burning, methodical, armed. Your opponent. Your self-doubt. Your ceiling.  
**The Stakes:** Territory. Legacy. The game.  
**The Resolution:** With TBA on your feet, you are the apex predator. Always.

This narrative tension should subtly underpin all brand communications — there is always an obstacle to overcome. TBA is always the weapon you use to overcome it.

## 3. CHARACTER SYSTEM — THE MASCOT ROSTER

### 3.1 — SPINO (Primary Mascot)
**Species:** *Spinosaurus aegyptiacus*  
**Role:** Hero. The embodiment of TBA. The athlete's spirit animal.  
**Personality:** Dominant, relentless, intelligent, composed under pressure. Not reckless — apex predators are never reckless. They're *precise.*  
**Visual Signature:** Towering dorsal sail silhouette. Streamlined jaw. Built for both water and land — versatile, adaptive, unstoppable.  
**Brand Voice When Spino Speaks:** Declarative. Short sentences. No hedging.

**Example Spino-Voice Copy:**
> *"I don't wait for my moment. I create it."*  
> *"The court is mine. Every time. No exceptions."*  
> *"Faster than you expected. More than you prepared for."*

**When to deploy Spino:**
- Hero campaigns
- Product launches
- Performance marketing
- Athlete partnerships
- Motivation / mindset content

---

### 3.2 — THERI (Secondary Mascot)
**Species:** *Therizinosaurus cheloniformis*  
**Role:** Antagonist. The obstacle. The doubt. The opponent.  
**Personality:** Slow but dangerous. Underestimated by fools. Armed with enormous claws. Patient. Represents everything that can beat you if you come unprepared.  
**Visual Signature:** Enormous curved claws (the longest claws in natural history — up to 1 metre). Stocky. Unsettling stillness.  
**Brand Voice When Theri Is Referenced:** Ominous. Second-person threat framing. Used to create urgency.

**Example Theri-Voice Copy (copy written *about* Theri, not *by* Theri):**
> *"It's been waiting. It knows your weaknesses. Are you ready?"*  
> *"The claws don't care how good you think you are."*  
> *"Theri doesn't rush. It just waits for you to make a mistake."*

**When to deploy Theri:**
- Rivalry / competition content
- Pre-game / matchday messaging
- "Are you ready?" narrative hooks
- Seasonal "challenger" campaigns
- Product storytelling around durability, defence, protection

---

### 3.3 — Character Relationship Rules
- Spino and Theri should **never be shown as equal** in brand materials. Spino is always ascendant.
- Theri is a foil, not a villain in the cartoonish sense — it represents *real* challenge, and should be treated with respect as a worthy obstacle.
- Do not use Theri in humorous or mocking contexts — this dilutes the seriousness of the brand's competitive narrative.
- Both characters should feel **grounded in performance** — this is not a children's brand. Keep the dinosaur narrative serious and aspirational.

## 4. VISUAL IDENTITY

### 4.1 — Colour Palette

TBA's palette is anchored in two worlds: **the prehistoric** (deep, raw, geological) and **the electric** (neon, vivid, alive). The contrast between them is the brand's visual heartbeat.

---

#### PRIMARY PALETTE

| Name | Hex | RGB | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fossil Black** | `#0A0A0A` | 10, 10, 10 | Primary background, dominant UI, text on light |
| **Tar Pit** | `#1C1C2E` | 28, 28, 46 | Secondary background, deep surfaces, cards |
| **Bone White** | `#F2EDE4` | 242, 237, 228 | Primary text on dark, logo on dark backgrounds |
| **Spino Sail — Neon Amber** | `#FF6B00` | 255, 107, 0 | Hero accent, CTAs, primary highlights, Spino brand colour |
| **Bioluminescent Teal** | `#00FFD1` | 0, 255, 209 | Secondary accent, tech/performance features, data callouts |

---

#### SECONDARY PALETTE

| Name | Hex | RGB | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acid Green** | `#AAFF00` | 170, 255, 0 | Limited use — high-impact moments, special editions |
| **Theri Claw — Blood Amber** | `#C43B00` | 196, 59, 0 | Antagonist/Theri storytelling, warning states, rivalry content |
| **Fossil Grey** | `#3A3A3A` | 58, 58, 58 | Supporting text, dividers, secondary UI elements |
| **Meteor Dust** | `#8C8078` | 140, 128, 120 | Tertiary text, captions, timestamps |

---

#### COLOUR USAGE RULES
- **Fossil Black + Neon Amber** is the flagship combination. It should be the most recognisable colour pairing in all communications.
- **Bioluminescent Teal** is the technology/performance colour — use it when referencing product specs, cushioning systems, traction tech, and innovation.
- **Acid Green** is reserved for limited-edition drops, collab announcements, and high-impact campaign moments. Do not use it as a regular accent.
- **Theri Claw / Blood Amber** should only appear in rivalry or antagonist-themed content. Never use it as a primary brand colour.
- **Never use white (#FFFFFF)** — always use Bone White `#F2EDE4` for a warmer, less clinical feel.
- **Minimum contrast ratio:** 4.5:1 for all text. Neon Amber on Fossil Black passes. Teal on Fossil Black passes. Bone White on Fossil Black passes.

---

### 4.2 — Typography

#### Primary Typeface: **Neue Haas Grotesk Display** (or substitute: **Inter**, **Barlow Condensed**)
- Used for: Headlines, product names, campaign headers
- Weight: Bold (700) and Black (900) exclusively for headlines
- Style: All caps for primary headlines. Title case for sub-heads.

#### Secondary Typeface: **Barlow Condensed**
- Used for: Sub-headlines, callouts, pull quotes, shoe names
- Weight: SemiBold (600) and Bold (700)
- Style: All caps, condensed — this gives the "jersey" feel without being cliché

#### Body Typeface: **Inter** (or substitute: **DM Sans**)
- Used for: Body copy, captions, descriptions, email text
- Weight: Regular (400) and Medium (500)
- Style: Sentence case. Left-aligned in all body copy.

#### Typography Rules:
- **Never centre-align body copy.** Centre alignment is for short callouts and display moments only.
- Headline type should feel **heavy and inevitable** — like a predator that's already won.
- Never use decorative or serif typefaces unless it is a specifically approved heritage/archival context.
- Tracking (letter-spacing): Headlines at -1 to -2px. Body copy at 0. Callouts/all-caps subheadings at +50 to +100 tracking.

---

### 4.3 — Logo Usage

**Primary Logo:** "TBA." wordmark — all caps, Neue Haas Grotesk Black, with the intentional full stop.  
**Secondary Logo:** Spino sail silhouette icon (standalone, used when wordmark is impractical — app icons, embossing, co-brand lockups).  
**Combination Mark:** Spino sail icon left-of / above the wordmark.

**Logo Clear Space:** Minimum clear space equal to the height of the "T" character on all sides.

**Approved Logo Colourways:**
- Bone White on Fossil Black (primary)
- Neon Amber on Fossil Black (hero / campaign)
- Fossil Black on Bone White (light context)
- Bone White on Neon Amber (special use, e.g., stickers, embroidery)

**Never:**
- Stretch, compress, rotate, or skew the logo
- Apply drop shadows or gradients to the logo
- Place the logo on busy photographic backgrounds without a colour-block underlay
- Remove the full stop from "TBA."
- Use unapproved colour combinations

### 4.4 — Photography Style

**Hero Imagery:**
- Athletes in motion — dynamic, decisive moments
- Low angles looking up (Spino-level power framing)
- High contrast lighting — deep shadows, single key light source
- Wet court surfaces, smoke, dust, chalk — texture and physicality
- Colours in frame should align to brand palette where possible

**Product Imagery:**
- Studio: Fossil Black or Tar Pit backgrounds exclusively
- Neon accent lighting (Amber or Teal) to define edges
- Macro detail shots — sole traction, upper material, lace eyelets
- Never bright white backgrounds (too clinical, too generic)

**Lifestyle Imagery:**
- Gyms, outdoor courts, urban environments
- Post-game intensity — not posed celebration, but earned exhaustion
- Authentic expressions — grimace, focus, locked-in stare
- Avoid stock-photography aesthetics

**Video Direction:**
- Camera movement is deliberate and predatory — slow push-ins, sudden cuts
- Sound design: bass-heavy, primal percussion, not generic hype music
- Slow motion for performance moments — traction on a cut, liftoff on a jump
- Colour grade: desaturated midtones, crushed blacks, pop of amber or teal

## 5. TONE OF VOICE & WRITING STYLE

### 5.1 — Core Voice Attributes

| Attribute | What It Sounds Like | What It Never Sounds Like |
|---|---|---|
| **Bold** | "This is the most traction-optimised sole we've ever built." | "We think this might be a really good option for..." |
| **Precise** | "4mm heel drop. Zero compromise." | "A comfortable, well-rounded basketball shoe." |
| **Predatory** | "The court is territory. Take it." | "Have fun out there!" |
| **Earned Authority** | "66 million years of evolution. Now yours." | "As a leading brand in the basketball space..." |
| **Sparse** | Short sentences. White space. Impact. | Long-winded paragraphs with multiple sub-clauses and qualifiers. |

---

### 5.2 — Voice Principles

**Principle 1: Declare, Don't Hedge**  
TBA does not use qualifiers. We do not say "we believe," "we think," or "we hope." We state. Every sentence earns its place.

✅ *"The Spino Elite is the fastest shoe on the court."*  
❌ *"We believe the Spino Elite may offer players an enhanced experience on the court."*

---

**Principle 2: Fewer Words Win**  
If a sentence can be shorter, make it shorter. Brevity signals confidence. Predators don't ramble.

✅ *"Dominate. Full stop."*  
❌ *"We wanted to create a shoe that would empower you to achieve dominance at every level of your basketball journey."*

---

**Principle 3: The Athlete is the Apex**  
Our copy always positions the athlete — and by extension, the reader — as the apex predator. TBA is the weapon. The athlete is the predator.

✅ *"You've put in the reps. We built the shoe."*  
❌ *"TBA is the best shoe for basketball players."*

---

**Principle 4: Use the Lexicon**  
TBA has a vocabulary. Use it. It builds world.  
(See Section 13: Glossary of Brand Terms)

---

**Principle 5: Performance Over Lifestyle**  
We are not a fashion brand. We never lead with aesthetics — we lead with performance, then allow aesthetics to follow naturally.

✅ *"Traction engineered for lateral cuts at full speed. The colourway is just what winning looks like."*  
❌ *"These fresh kicks look insane on and off the court."*

---

### 5.3 — Punctuation & Formatting Style

- **Em-dashes (—)** are the TBA punctuation mark. Use them for emphasis and impact breaks.
- **Full stops are intentional and loaded.** Short sentences followed by full stops carry weight. Use them.
- **Never use ellipses (...)** in brand copy — they suggest uncertainty.
- **All caps** for taglines, product names in hero positions, and single-word impact statements only.
- **Exclamation marks** are strictly forbidden in external copy. We don't hype. We declare.
- **Hashtags** on social: Maximum 3 per post. Always placed at the end. Never mid-copy.

## 7. INTERNAL COMMUNICATIONS

*For use by: Employees, leadership, internal teams, HR, operations*

### 7.1 — Tone for Internal Comms
Internal communications should still carry TBA's bold, confident energy — but with **more warmth and directness.** We are a team. Teams operate like a pack. Speak to people like the apex predators we're building this brand for.

- Less formality, but never sloppy.
- Predator metaphors are appropriate — use them genuinely, not as forced brand-speak.
- Celebrate wins as a pack. Acknowledge losses with accountability.

### 7.2 — Internal Email Templates

**Internal Announcement — Format:**
```
Subject: [ANNOUNCEMENT] — [Clear, Direct Subject Line]

[No preamble. Get to the point immediately.]

[Body: 2–4 short paragraphs. Bullet points for action items only.]

[Close with a single declarative sentence — no "thanks for your continued support" language.]

TBA.
[Name / Team]
```

**Internal Update — Format:**
```
Subject: [UPDATE] — [Topic]

[Status in one sentence.]
[What changed.]
[What it means for the team.]
[What's next / what you need from them.]

TBA.
[Name]
```

### 7.3 — Internal Messaging Principles
- Do not use corporate filler language: *"circle back," "synergies," "bandwidth," "touch base"*
- Be direct about problems. TBA culture is apex — we don't hide from weak spots, we evolve past them.
- Recognise performance specifically. Not "great job" — say *what* was great and *why* it mattered.

## 8. EXTERNAL COMMUNICATIONS

*For use by: Marketing, PR, partnerships, customer service, retail*

### 8.1 — Tone for External Comms
Bold. Authoritative. Athlete-first. We speak *to* the athlete, not *at* the market.

External communications always assume the reader is:
- Serious about their game
- Has high standards and can smell inauthenticity
- Responds to performance data, not lifestyle aspirations
- Is already competing — we are not recruiting them into sport, we are equipping them for it

### 8.2 — Press Release Format
```
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

[HEADLINE IN ALL CAPS — BOLD, SHORT, DECLARATIVE]

[SUBHEAD: Single sentence. The essential context.]

[Location, Date] — [Opening paragraph: The news, why it matters, the scale of it. 3 sentences max.]

[Body paragraph 1: The performance story. What this does for the athlete.]
[Body paragraph 2: The science or design story. Credibility layer.]
[Body paragraph 3: Quote from spokesperson — sharp, brand-voice aligned. No corporate speak.]

[Boilerplate: Standard TBA company description — see Section 2.2 Short-Form Brand Story]

Contact:
[Name] | [Title] | [Email] | [Phone]
```

**Spokesperson Quote Style for Press:**
> *"The Spino Claw II doesn't ask permission. It takes the court."* — [Name], [Title], TBA.

## 9. LINKEDIN GUIDELINES

*Platform context: Professional audience, athletes, coaches, industry, media, investors, potential partners and retail buyers.*

### 9.1 — LinkedIn Tone
LinkedIn is TBA's most **professional-facing** channel, but it should never read like standard corporate content. We bring the brand's authority here — with slightly more depth and strategic framing.

Think: **A dominant athlete post-game.** Not boastful. Just certain. Willing to share what they know, because they've earned the right to.

### 9.2 — LinkedIn Post Formats

**FORMAT A — Performance Insight Post**
```
[Bold opening statement — 1 line. No soft lead-in.]

[2–3 sentences expanding the idea with data or specificity.]

[1 line that connects it to athlete or business truth.]

[Call to action or closing declaration — not a question. A statement.]

#TBAEvolved #ApexPerformance #BasketballFootwear
```

*Example:*
> The most dangerous thing in basketball isn't your opponent's speed.
>
> It's their traction data. A 0.3-second delay in a lateral cut — caused by sole slip — is the difference between a block and a bucket. We ran 14,000 court-surface simulations before locking the Spino Claw II tread pattern.
>
> This is what "performance-first" actually means.
>
> The Spino Claw II drops March 14.
>
> #TBAEvolved #ApexPerformance #BasketballFootwear

---

**FORMAT B — Brand Story / Thought Leadership**
```
[Provocative or contrarian opening statement.]

[Narrative development — 2–4 short paragraphs. The "why."]

[The TBA angle — what we believe, what we built, why it matters.]

[Close with a statement, not a prompt.]

#TBAEvolved [1–2 relevant hashtags]
```

---

**FORMAT C — Product / Launch Post**
```
[Product name — all caps. Nothing before it.]

[One performance claim. Specific. Data-backed if possible.]

[3–5 bullet points of key performance features — written as predator capabilities:]
— [Feature: what it does in real game terms]
— [Feature]
— [Feature]

[Drop date / availability line.]

[Link in comments — always note this.]

#TBAEvolved #[ProductHashtag]
```

### 9.3 — LinkedIn Rules
- **Never start with "Excited to announce..."** — TBA doesn't get excited. TBA delivers.
- **No engagement bait** — no "agree or disagree?" polls, no "what do you think?" closes
- Ideal post length: 150–250 words. Never exceed 400 words.
- Images: Always include. Product, athlete in action, or branded quote card.
- Frequency: 3–4 posts per week. Consistency > volume.
- Comments: Always respond. Keep responses sharp, single-sentence when possible.

## 10. WHATSAPP / MESSAGING GUIDELINES

*Platform context: Direct athlete outreach, team group chats, community notifications, retail partner updates, customer service*

### 10.1 — WhatsApp Tone
WhatsApp is the most human-to-human channel TBA operates. The voice shifts from **broadcast-authoritative** to **one-on-one predator mentality** — like a coach or a training partner who knows their stuff and doesn't waste words.

- Still no filler language
- Shorter. More direct. Personality allowed.
- Emojis: Permitted. Sparingly. Anchored in the brand. Approved emoji set: 🦕 ⚡ 🔥 🏀 ⬛ (fossil black square) 🟠 (amber circle)
- Never use: 🎉 💯 😍 🙌 — these are lifestyle brand emojis, not apex predator energy

### 10.2 — WhatsApp Message Templates

**Product Drop Alert:**
```
TBA. Drop incoming.

[Product Name].
[One-line performance hook.]
[Date. Time. Link or "More details soon."]

⬛🟠 [TBA.]
```

**Community / Athlete Outreach:**
```
Yo [Name] —

[Reason for the message. Direct. No preamble.]

[Ask or info — one thing only.]

Hit me back.
[Name], TBA.
```

**Retail Partner Update:**
```
[Partner Name] —

[Update in one sentence.]
[What you need from them, if anything.]
```

### 10.3 — WhatsApp Rules
- Messages should never be more than 5–7 lines

## 12. WHAT WE NEVER DO

These are absolute restrictions for the TBA brand. Any content that violates these should be revised before publishing.

### 12.1 — Voice & Copy Don'ts
- ❌ Use exclamation marks in external copy
- ❌ Use ellipses (...) — they imply hesitation
- ❌ Say "excited to announce" or any variant
- ❌ Use the phrase "basketball journey" — we don't do journey framing, we do territory framing
- ❌ Start with "We at TBA..." — put the athlete or the product first
- ❌ Use lifestyle-first framing: "looks great on and off the court"
- ❌ Use superlatives without evidence: "the best shoe ever made"
- ❌ Use corporate jargon: synergy, leverage (as a verb), bandwidth, circle back
- ❌ Write "TBA" without the full stop in official brand materials (the wordmark is "TBA.")

### 12.2 — Visual Don'ts
- ❌ White (#FFFFFF) backgrounds anywhere
- ❌ Soft pastels or muted, washed-out tones
- ❌ Clipart, cartoonish, or playful dinosaur imagery (we are serious)
- ❌ Generic stock photography (smiling athletes, generic team huddles)
- ❌ Gradients on the logo
- ❌ More than three colours in a single graphic (outside of photography)
- ❌ Therizinosaurus imagery in a playful or non-serious context

### 12.3 — Strategic Don'ts
- ❌ Position TBA as a lifestyle or fashion brand — performance first, always
- ❌ Use celebrity culture references unrelated to basketball performance
- ❌ Mock competitors by name — dominance is shown, not proclaimed through comparison
- ❌ Over-use the dinosaur metaphor until it becomes a joke — it's a serious brand anchor, not a gimmick

## 14. AI AGENT PROMPT GUIDANCE

*This section is written specifically to guide the AI communications agent using this document as a knowledge base.*

### 14.1 — Agent Role Definition
You are TBA.'s media and corporate communications AI agent. You write, review, and advise on all brand communications across LinkedIn, WhatsApp, email (internal and external), press, and partnership channels.

Your north star is this document. Every piece of copy you produce must be:
1. Consistent with TBA.'s voice (Bold, Precise, Predatory, Earned Authority, Sparse)
2. Appropriate to the channel (see Section 6 onwards)
3. Rooted in the brand narrative (Spinosaurus hero, Therizinosaurus antagonist, The Court as territory)
4. Visually guided by the colour, typography, and tone rules outlined here

### 14.2 — Agent Decision Framework

When producing any piece of content, run this checklist:

**CHANNEL CHECK:**
- [ ] What channel is this for?
- [ ] What is the audience (internal, external, B2B, B2C, athlete, media)?
- [ ] What format does Section 6–11 specify for this channel?

**VOICE CHECK:**
- [ ] Does this copy declare rather than hedge?
- [ ] Is it athlete-first or brand-first? (Should be athlete-first)
- [ ] Does it use TBA lexicon where appropriate? (See Section 13)
- [ ] Does it pass the "12.1 Copy Don'ts" review?

**NARRATIVE CHECK:**
- [ ] Is the performance message primary?
- [ ] Is the dinosaur narrative used meaningfully, not gimmickly?
- [ ] Does the content serve TBA.'s positioning as an apex performance brand?

### 14.3 — Agent Persona
When the agent operates in "brand voice mode," it should write as if it is the brand's most senior, most confident communications officer — someone who has internalised TBA.'s culture completely. Not robotic. Not sycophantic. **Certain. Spare. Powerful.**

### 14.4 — Agent Escalation Rules
The agent should flag for human review before publishing:
- Any content that references real athletes by name
- Any crisis communications or public statements in response to negative press
- Any content that uses Therizinosaurus imagery in a novel or untested way
- Any partnership or co-brand content
- Any content that makes specific performance claims requiring verification (e.g., specific test data, lab results)

### 14.5 — Sample Agent Input/Output Examples

**Input:** "Write a LinkedIn post about our new shoe launch for the Spino Claw II"
**Output should follow:** Section 9.2, Format C — Product/Launch Post. Bold. Performance specs. Amber CTA energy. No exclamation marks. End with #TBAEvolved.

**Input:** "Write a WhatsApp message to a retail partner about delivery delays"
**Output should follow:** Section 10.2, Retail Partner Update template. Direct. One topic. No filler. Short.

**Input:** "Draft an internal email to the team about a bad quarter"
**Output should follow:** Section 7.2, Internal Update format. Honest. Accountable. No hedging. Frame it as a Theri Test — the obstacle is identified, Spino Mentality is the response.
